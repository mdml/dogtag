The two drift chapters, read slowly, which is the only speed they permit.

The first is the essay that started all of this for me three winters ago, revised for the collection: the beach as archive, the wrack line as annotation, the walker as the instrument that no one has yet calibrated. Reading it again with two seasons of my own ledger behind me is a strange experience — half the sentences have become obvious because I have lived them, and the other half have become questions because I have lived their exceptions. Her cork lines drift west; mine drift east; we are on opposite shores of the same argument and neither of us gets to be the control group.

The second chapter is new, and it is the one I will be quoting for years. She takes the method inland — drift mapping a river that no longer exists, from the debris fields it left in hedgerows and deed maps and the diagonal stubbornness of one parish boundary. The claim underneath, never quite stated: an absence with enough consequences is a presence, and can be surveyed like one. Plate 4 wants this sentence. The foreword, if she writes it, will apparently be an apology for the whole method, delivered from inside it, which is the most Odile-shaped structure imaginable.

Marginalia count: eleven, a personal record for forty-eight pages. Stopping here for the night because reading the closing essay tired would waste it.
