# Quince and Rye Galette

The answer to the annual glut, arrived at after two soggy failures and one burnt one. The rye is not optional: quince against a plain wheat crust is sweet on sweet, and the sourness of the rye is what makes the whole thing worth eating.

## The quinces

Peel, quarter, core — accept that this takes twenty minutes and cannot be rushed, quinces being armoured. Poach the quarters in barely simmering water with the juice of the lemon and a spoon of honey until they turn from pale to that improbable coral, forty minutes or so. Lift them out and reduce the poaching liquid to a syrup. All of this happily happens a day ahead, and the colour deepens overnight.

## The crust

Both flours, a good pinch of salt, then the cold butter rubbed in coarsely — stop earlier than feels right, the visible butter is what gives the shatter. Bring it together with as little iced water as it will accept, rest it an hour.

## Assembly

Roll to a rough circle the size of an accusation, about 30 cm. Slice the poached quarters thickly and shingle them from the centre out, leaving a wide border. Fold the border over in blunt pleats; it should look confident rather than tidy. Scatter the demerara over fruit and crust both.

Hot oven, 200, on a preheated tray, 40 to 45 minutes, until the pleats are deeply browned — undercooked rye crust is penance, so err long. Brush the fruit with the reduced syrup the moment it comes out.

Milo's verdict on the first good one: "you could sell this," which from him is either high praise or an accusation of ambition. Serves eight, or four allotment people who dug that morning.
