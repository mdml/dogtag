# Field Atlas Planning Session

Kitchen table, two hours, one pot of coffee that did not survive the paper discussion.

**Plate order.** Tamsin walked the press queue through October. Plates 5 and 6 share a steel-facing run if both are drawn by September; otherwise they split and the schedule loses three weeks. Agreed: 5 and 6 by September, plate 8 slips to first reserve rather than being promised.

**Paper.** The May proofs on the lighter stock cockled along the plate edge. Tamsin's position, held firmly and correctly, is that a cockled atlas is a ruined atlas and the heavier laid stock is the only defensible choice. The cost difference is real — roughly a fifth of the remaining budget — and was accepted without much grief. Decision recorded as final; revisiting it is banned until binding.

**Survey dates.** June brought the tide tables and did the thing she does where the whole autumn resolves into four usable low-water windows in about ninety seconds. The September window is the good one: morning lows, workable light. The October window is the fallback and clashes with the members' show setup, so September it must be.

**Other business.** Milo offered the allotment shed as flat storage for finished prints, which solves a problem I had been politely ignoring. [[Ruben Askew]] has the long ladder if the winch-platform photographs need retaking from the upper path — asked in passing, agreed in passing, should be confirmed properly.

Next session pencilled for late August, after plates 1 through 4 go to Odile.
