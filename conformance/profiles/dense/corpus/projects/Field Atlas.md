# Field Atlas

Eight etched plates, printed at [[Brindlewood Cooperative]], each pairing a surveyed edge of the Hollow with its drift ribbon — the [[Drift Mapping]] treatment worked up from two years of ledger walks. One plate a month through November, then binding over the winter.

## Plates

1. [[Gullwing Point]] — anchor plate; ledger is deepest here. Proofed in May, reworked after the state-two proof showed the stipple field reading as mud rather than probability.
2. The winch platform edge, 2019 against now — the erosion pair.
3. Saltmarsh channels at low water.
4. The dry channel above the mill — owes everything to the winterbourne chapter of [[A Field Guide to Vanishing Rivers]].
5. Shingle sorting bands, spring versus neap.
6. The harbour mouth, charted line only — the control plate, deliberately without a ribbon.
7. The allotment stream culvert, the one inland plate.
8. The whole Hollow at a spread, ribbon only, no line — the argument plate.

## Working notes

[[Tamsin Reyes]] has the press queue mapped through October and says plates 5 and 6 can share a steel-facing run if I get both drawn by September. The paper decision is made: the heavier laid stock, despite the cost, after the May proofs cockled.

[[Odile Krantz]] has agreed to write a short foreword if she can see plates 1 through 4 by early autumn, which is now the real deadline whatever the frontmatter says.

Risks, honestly held: the stipple-ribbon convention may only read to me (see the objections collected in the drift-mapping note); plate 2 depends on photographs I do not own the rights to yet; and November printing at the cooperative collides with the members' show. The fallback is six plates and a thinner atlas, which would still be a finished thing rather than a beautiful abandonment.
