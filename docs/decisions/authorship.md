# Authorship: attribution derives from history; ownership is enforced policy; in-band marking is presentation

- **Status:** adopted — interviewed, drafted, and adopted 2026-07-29 (batched interview + open discussion)
- **Decided / updated:** 2026-07-29
- **Layer:** both — the substrate stamps, derives, and enforces; surfaces render voice and respect ownership
- **Config vs. invariant:** *Invariant* — every span of a multi-hand corpus has an **answerable authorship question**, and the answer **derives from the version record** — attribution is never hand-maintained metadata; the write path **stamps actor identity** (and provenance kind) at commit time; attribution **granularity scales with commit discipline and degrades gracefully** with history quality — coarse truth, never false precision; **provenance kinds imply edit rules**; the substrate **always knows who may write where** (per-type ownership policy plus within-note carve-outs) and its write verbs refuse violations; per-note summary authorship values are **computed, never authored**. *Config-point* — the provenance-kind vocabulary; which actor boundaries a vault distinguishes (human↔AI only, or per-person); ownership-policy assignments per type; carve-out markup; **in-band attribution marking (default: off)** — a per-vault presentation choice, always a claim verifiable against history, never the record; granularity conventions (e.g. one-line-per-paragraph authoring, which makes line-level attribution paragraph-level for free).

> **Boundary.** [git-integration](git-integration.md) provides the substrate this PDR builds on — the attribution grammar carrying full actor identity, and the merge posture that preserves it; that PDR filed the "can history replace in-document fencing" question here, and this PDR answers it. [markdown-flavor](markdown-flavor.md)'s round-trip contract becomes load-bearing for a second reason here (see Tradeoffs); [note-types](note-types.md) supplies the type key that ownership policy hangs on. This PDR owns what authorship *means*: span attribution, actor kinds, provenance semantics, and write-rights.

## 👁️ Intent

A knowledge corpus is written by many hands — its owner across years, agents writing on their behalf, co-authors — and two trust questions follow it everywhere. The reader's: *whose voice is this?* Prose generated for me and prose authored by me (or by a named collaborator) carry different weight, and a corpus that can't tell them apart at a glance erodes exactly the trust that makes it worth consulting. And the writer's: *may I write here?* Some prose must remain one hand's — a journal no agent touches, a reflection no synthesis overwrites — and that boundary is only real if the system enforces it rather than requesting it. Both answers must be cheap enough to be universal: attribution anyone maintains by hand is attribution that decays.

## 🎨 Design

**Attribution derives from the version record.** Every write already passes the commit boundary carrying its actor's identity and its provenance kind ([git-integration](git-integration.md)'s commit standard); authorship is therefore *derivable* rather than *declarable* — the substrate answers "who wrote this span, under what kind" by resolving the version record, the same way it answers a link's identity or a note's backlinks: a derived verb, computed once, consumed by every surface. Derived attribution has the property no authored marking can have: it cannot drift. There is no reconciliation debt, no marker that lies after an edit, no discipline that must hold across every writer — the write is the record.

**Granularity scales with commit discipline — and degrades gracefully.** History-derived attribution resolves as finely as the commits that made it: a corpus written under scoped, per-actor, commit-at-birth discipline yields effectively per-line attribution (and a one-line-per-paragraph authoring convention makes that per-paragraph for free); a corpus with coarse history — bulk imports, a host-owned workflow, squashed ancestry — yields per-file or per-era attribution. Both answers are *true*; discipline buys resolution. This is the deliberate composition of the two PDRs: the commit posture is what makes fine-grained authorship free, and fine-grained authorship is part of why the posture is worth keeping.

**Provenance kinds imply edit rules.** Actor identity says who; the provenance kind says *what kind of writing it was* — faithfully formalized from a source, extended in dialog, synthesized across existing notes — and each kind implies what a later writer may do with the span (stay faithful to the source; extend in the conversational direction; re-derive). Kinds are stamped at write time in the commit standard and derived per-span like actor identity. The kind vocabulary is config; the invariant is that kinds carry edit semantics — a kind that tells the next writer nothing is decoration.

**Ownership is policy, and the substrate enforces it.** Orthogonal to *who wrote* is *who may write*. Every note type carries an ownership policy (human-only, machine-only, mixed), and within a mixed note the author may carve out protected regions; the substrate knows both and its write verbs refuse violations — ownership is enforcement, not etiquette. Policy hangs on the type system; carve-outs are the author's, and their concrete markup is config.

**In-band marking is presentation, not the record — and defaults off.** A vault may choose to also mark authorship in the file itself (a fence around machine prose, for plain-text readers). The product's stance: such marks are *claims* — renderable, verifiable against the derived truth, flaggable when they disagree — never the attribution record, and the shipped default is not to write them. What in-band marking buys (legibility in the bare file) is real but narrow; what it costs — markup burden that caps granularity, a second copy of a derivable fact, and a standing drift-reconciliation debt — recurs on every edit forever.

## ⚖️ Tradeoffs

- **Rejected: in-band fences as the attribution record.** Authored span markers fail on four fronts at once. They drift — a human edit inside a marked span makes the marker lie, creating a permanent reconciliation chore. They tax the author — which is why real corpora mark only the coarsest boundary and attribution stays poor. They cap granularity — per-sentence, per-actor marking of co-authored prose is unreadable markup no one sustains, while the version record carries it for free. And they are a second copy of a derivable fact, which is the definition of a consistency liability. Marking survives only as the optional presentation layer above.
- **Rejected: hand-maintained authorship metadata** (a frontmatter value each writer must set). Discipline-based correctness breaks at scale — every new writing tool and every new agent is one more place the discipline must hold; the audit that catches violations is itself standing work. Computed-from-the-record makes the signal structural. *(Receipt: the incumbent corpus lived its own frontmatter-authorship era and replaced it — the summary value had already been made computed.)*
- **Rejected: attribution without a version substrate.** There is no cheaper honest source: anything else re-invents a history mechanism piecemeal (edit logs, author stamps) without the merge, integrity, and tooling properties version control already has. This is why [git-integration](git-integration.md) names version control the prerequisite for multi-author — a corpus that wants authorship answers must be versioned; an unversioned or history-poor corpus gets proportionally coarse answers, honestly labeled.
- **Rejected: in-band marking default-on.** The bare-file reader loses the authorship signal — a real cost to plain-text legibility, accepted deliberately: that property's core value is *repair*, reading surfaces are tooled, and the computed per-note summary can persist in the metadata plane as a coarse in-file signal. Vaults that weigh bare-file reading higher flip the config.
- **Tension carried — attribution follows the mover.** Line-level derivation attributes a reflowed, reformatted, or relocated span to whoever moved it; move-detection heuristics and stable authoring conventions (soft-wrap) mitigate but don't eliminate. Accepted: occasionally-coarse truth beats markers that confidently lie.
- **Tension carried — the round-trip contract is now load-bearing for authorship.** A surface that rewrites bytes it didn't semantically touch doesn't just churn diffs — it *takes authorship* of every line it re-serializes. [markdown-flavor](markdown-flavor.md)'s contract gains a second enforcement reason; a non-conforming editor degrades attribution for everything it saves.

## 🖥️ Surfaces

The derivation, the commit standard, and the enforcement are substrate; surfaces render voice and honor rights.

- **CLI / SDK (the write path).** Already actor-typed and scoped per the commit posture — every agent write lands as its own attributed commit; the trailer standard (actor identity + provenance kind, machine-parseable) is the named follow-on that completes the record.
- **TUI (reading).** Voice rendering once the attribution index exists — a whose-voice gutter or span tinting, driven by the derived verb; per-note summary in the header meanwhile.
- **MCP.** Attribution as query and return fields — an agent checks a note's ownership policy and carve-outs *before* writing (refusal is substrate-side regardless), and can scope reads to a voice ("only my owner's prose").
- **Tolaria (GUI fallback).** The cautionary case twice over: its serializer's whole-file rewrites are exactly the authorship-stealing failure the round-trip contract exists to prevent, and its rendering of in-band markers as literal text is part of why marking-as-presentation is config, not contract.

## Notes

- **Interview record:** 2026-07-29, batched rounds plus an open discussion for the load-bearing question. The jobs ranked: **reader trust and ownership enforcement are the firm core**; agent edit-rules ride the kind vocabulary. The actor model supports *both* boundaries — human↔AI and person↔person ("it is helpful when co-authoring to be able to tell who wrote what sentence"). The decision argument, the maintainer's own: history-canonical attribution is *more powerful* (per-line down to per-file resolution depending on commit convention — "you have history") and *more automatable* than fencing; the only hesitation was engineering (efficient span indexing), explicitly separated from the product question.
- **Relationship to the fence model:** the incumbent corpus's fence-based authorship mechanics remain its operating model until a migration lands — that era's computed-frontmatter move and its kinds-imply-edit-rules insight are carried forward here; its in-band fences are what this PDR demotes to default-off presentation. The transition (trailer standard, fence retirement or conversion, tooling updates) is substrate work for its own ADR when pulled — this PDR records the product direction, not the migration.
- **Substrate follow-ons (recorded, not built):** the commit-trailer standard (actor identity + kind, machine-parseable — extends git-integration's grammar); the **attribution index** (efficient span-level derivation — blame-shaped, incremental, rebuildable like the search index; the efficiency question is open and was named so in the interview); carve-out representation once fences default off; the per-note computed summary's home in the metadata plane.
